Imagine you and your friends open a pizza shop

You need ₹10,00,000 to start — buy ovens, tables, staff. You have ₹2,00,000. What do you do?

You ask 8 friends to invest. Each gives ₹1,00,000. In return, each friend gets a share — a small piece of ownership in your pizza shop.

You own 20%

Because you put in ₹2L out of ₹10L. If the shop earns ₹1L profit, you get ₹20,000.

Each friend owns 10%

Put in ₹1L each. If the shop earns ₹1L profit, they each get ₹10,000.

That's exactly what a stock is

When a big company like Reliance or TCS needs money to grow, they sell small pieces of ownership to the public. Each piece is called a stock or share. You buy it → you own a tiny part of that company.

Real example: Infosys has about 415 crore shares outstanding. If you buy 1 share at ~₹1,500, you own 1/41,50,00,00,000 of Infosys. Small, but real ownership!

Why would a company give away ownership?

🔧 To get money for building new factories, products, offices
🌍 To expand to new cities or countries
💰 To repay loans without paying interest
⭐ To become famous and trusted (being "listed" is prestigious)

Two ways to make money from stocks

📈 Price goes up (Capital Gain)

Buy at ₹100, sell at ₹150 → profit ₹50

💵 Dividends

Company shares profit with you every year, even if you don't sell

How a trade actually happens

1
You open Zerodha / Groww / Upstox app and place a buy order for 10 shares of TCS at ₹3,500
2
Your broker sends the order to NSE/BSE electronically
3
NSE's computer finds someone willing to sell 10 TCS shares at ₹3,500
4
Trade is matched in milliseconds! Shares move to your Demat account in T+1 day

What is an Index? (SENSEX, NIFTY)

You can't track 5,000 companies daily. So we track a sample — a basket of the biggest, most important companies. Their average is the "index".

SENSEX

Top 30 companies on BSE. If SENSEX is up 1%, those 30 companies are worth ~1% more on average.

NIFTY 50

Top 50 companies on NSE. More diversified. Most traders watch this.

Think of it like a cricket team's average score. If the team average rises, the team is doing well — even if some players scored 0.

Price = what someone is willing to pay right now

There's no "correct" price for a stock. It's purely what buyers and sellers agree on at any moment. Like an auction.

What makes more people want to buy?

✅ Company announces huge profits
✅ New product launch / big contract won
✅ Government policy helps their industry
✅ Economy is growing fast, low interest rates
✅ Famous investor buys large stake

What makes more people want to sell?

❌ Company misses profit expectations
❌ Scandal, fraud, CEO resigns suddenly
❌ Competitor launches better product
❌ RBI raises interest rates (money gets expensive)
❌ War, pandemic, natural disaster

Psychology matters too — massively

Sometimes stocks move not because of facts, but because people feel scared or excited. This is called market sentiment. Fear can crash a stock even if the company is fine. Greed can pump a stock beyond its real value.

Example: In 2020, Zoom's stock rose 500% because people were excited about video calls during COVID, even though the company wasn't worth that much.

Support & Resistance — the two magic lines

Support level 🛡️

A price where the stock keeps "bouncing up" when it falls to that level. Think of it as a floor.

Resistance level 🧱

A price where the stock keeps "bouncing down" when it rises to that level. Think of it as a ceiling.

Why these animals?

A bull attacks by thrusting its horns upward → prices go up. A bear attacks by swiping its paws downward → prices go down. Simple visual memory trick.

Market cycle — what usually happens

The market moves in cycles. After every crash (bear), there has historically always been a recovery and new highs (bull). The trick is not to panic-sell at the bottom.

Other market moods

TermMeaning
CrashVery sudden, sharp drop (10%+ in days)
Correction10–20% drop, normal and healthy
Sideways / RangingPrices bouncing between support & resistance — no clear direction
RallySharp price rise, often after bad news passes

Circuit breakers — market's safety valve

If NIFTY falls more than 10% in a day, NSE pauses trading for 45 minutes. At 15%, pause is 1 hour 45 min. At 20%, market closes for the day. This prevents panic from spiraling.

Market Capitalisation (Market Cap)

Total value of a company = Share price × Total number of shares

P/E Ratio — is it expensive?

P/E = Share Price ÷ Earnings Per Share

Tells you: "For every ₹1 the company earns, how much are you paying?"

Price: ₹500
EPS: ₹25
P/E Ratio: 20
Average valuation
P/E below 15 = cheap; 15–25 = fair; above 25 = expensive (or fast-growing)

Dividend — passive income from stocks

Companies share a portion of profits with shareholders. Example: HDFC Bank pays ₹19.50 per share as dividend each year. Own 100 shares → get ₹1,950/year just for holding!

Dividend Yield
Dividend ÷ Price
Example
₹20 ÷ ₹500 = 4%
